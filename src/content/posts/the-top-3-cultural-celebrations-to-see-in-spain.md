When I travel, I really enjoy immersing myself in the cultural experiences of other countries. It's so exciting and interesting to learn about customs that are different to what I'm used to back in the U.S. One of the cultural differences I've learned about are the types of celebrations they have in Spain. No, there's no 4th of July or Thanksgiving, but they do have some pretty unique and fun celebrations of their own here.

## Carnival

This festival is usually held in February or March, in the weeks leading up to lent each year. Everyone dresses up in extravagant costumes and you can watch performances both on stage and in the streets. Cadiz has one of the most well-known carnivals in Spain, with a focus on costumes and performances centered around humor and irony. Almost all cities in Spain will have their own version of carnival though, so you can experience the excitement no matter where you are in the country.

![Carnival celebration](/images/spain-carnival.jpg)

## Holy Week

Also known as Semana Santa, holy week takes place the week of Easter each year. During this week, processions are held in the streets as they make their way to the cathedrals. While it's rooted in religious background, it is upheld as a tradition that Spaniards enjoy to partake in each year, even if they don't practice the religion. Some of the attire may remind you of a cult-like group, but you can rest assured there is no connection. It's simply the traditional attire that's been worn, dating back thousands of years ago. One of the most popular holy week celebrations takes place in Sevilla, where people travel all over the world just to watch these processions.

## Feria

Feria (translated fair in English) is an annual festival in Spain that takes place at each city's local fairgrounds. It reminds me of a state fair, but with a Spanish flair. You'll find everything from fair foods and rides, to flamenco dancing and bullfights. You'll also find a lot of locals wearing traditional flamenco costumes, but don't let this deter you as it's not a requirement to join the fun. The most popular feria takes place in Sevilla in April, but you can find one in almost every city in Spain between the months of April and August.
