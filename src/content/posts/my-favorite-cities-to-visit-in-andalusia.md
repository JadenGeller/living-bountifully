Andalusia is one of the largest regions in Spain, located in the southern-most part of the country. The region's Moorish heritage influences its architecture, and its diverse geography means visitors can experience everything from rolling hillsides with farmland in Jerez, dry dessert terrain near Almeria and snowy mountainous areas like Granada.

## Sevilla

This is my favorite destination and the region's capital. The city is filled with a ton of history and Moorish influence visible in the Royal Alcazar architecture. It hosts the best Semana Santa processions during Easter week and Feria celebrations in April. I recommend visitors experience traditional flamenco performances here.

![Plaza de España in Sevilla](/images/andalusia-sevilla.jpg)

## Ronda

A smaller town ideal for day trips or overnight stays, Ronda features the Puente Nuevo bridge connecting old and new districts. The region is known for it's olive oil, with tastings recommended.

![Ronda bridge](/images/andalusia-ronda.jpg)

## Cadiz

An ancient port city, Cadiz features over 100 watchtowers that were originally built to keep watch and signal to incoming ships. The Tavira Tower offers a 360 degree view of the city. The city hosts one of the best carnivals in Spain, which takes place in the weeks leading up to lent each year.

![Cadiz coastline](/images/andalusia-cadiz.jpg)

## Jerez de la Frontera

Known as the sherry capital of the world, Jerez sits within the Sherry Triangle, the source of all sherry grapes. If it's not produced in the Sherry Triangle, it's not sherry! The city is also well-known for its equestrian school and horse shows.

![Jerez architecture](/images/andalusia-jerez.jpg)

## Malaga

I experienced so much energy in this city, with attractions for various interests. History enthusiasts can visit the Alcazar, Cathedral, and Roman theatre, plus museums including the Picasso Museum. Culture-focused travelers might explore the Atarazanas Market or take tapas tours. December visitors encounter notable Christmas displays—there's nothing like walking down the streets at night with lights flickering all around you.

![Malaga city](/images/andalusia-malaga.jpg)

## Marbella

A gem on the Mediterranean Sea, Marbella features streets that look like a movie and ranks among Spain's more luxurious travel destinations. It's characterized by big yachts, fancy cars, and designer stores, plus numerous golf courses. Activities include wandering streets or relaxing beachside.

![Marbella streets](/images/andalusia-marbella.jpg)

## Nerja

An oceanfront beach town only about an hour from Malaga, Nerja is most known for its caves, which you can tour. The Balcony of Europe viewpoint provides stunning panoramic views of the coastline.

![Nerja coastline](/images/andalusia-nerja.jpg)
