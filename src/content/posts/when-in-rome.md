Known as the city of love, food and Roman ruins (to me, at least), Rome is certainly a mighty city to behold. I don't think I was prepared for just how big this city really is, and how much there is to see and do while you're here. I was graced by this city's presence for only a weekend, so I imagine I'll have some editing to do if, and when, I return in the future. In the meantime, here's a highlight reel of top things to see when in Rome…

While the **Vatican** and **Necropolis** are *technically* not in Rome, they are definitely a top tourist attraction while you're here. And you'll know it! Almost as amazing as the grandeur of the basilica are the hoards of people waiting in line to see it. These are definitely tours you'll want to buy tickets in advanced for.

**Castel Sant'Angelo** is a beautiful castle situated on the Tiber River, and its unique entrance over an ancient bridge crossing the river makes the castle feel even more grand.

The **Pantheon,** another well known site, is nestled in narrows streets. It certainly took me by surprise when I turned the corner to see it towering over me in all it's glory.

I have to admit, I'd never heard of or even seen pictures of the **Altare della Patria** monument until it smacked me in the face while walking down the street. All I could think was HOW have I not heard of this before?! It's a magnificent monument and definitely worth the stop to get that "influencer" shot.

![Altare della Patria](/images/rome-altare.jpg)

There is no walk to the Colosseum more enjoyable than through the **Roman Forum**. I was literally surrounded by Roman ruins on either side of me; I didn't know which way to look!

![Roman Forum](/images/rome-forum.jpg)

Speaking of the **Colosseum**, this is probably what you think of when you think of Rome. I know it was for me, at least. Well, that and lots of wine and carbs. I've seen a few Roman theaters in my travels, but nothing as grand and well preserved as the Colosseum. It's… *Colossal.* Bad dad joke, sorry!

I can't believe I made it this far without mentioning the **Trevi Fountain**. It's quite literally the fountain that dreams are made of. And it really is magical if you can get past the hoards of people trying to get their perfect "I'm the only one here" photo. Nothing like an over-population of tourists to spoil a good historical monument. Oh wait, I guess I'm one too.

![Trevi Fountain](/images/rome-trevi.jpg)

Not as well known, **Piazza Navona** is a beautiful plaza with three interesting and grand fountains showcased there. It's a great place to stop for some gelato, a drink or a bite to eat.

Last but not least, the **Jewish Quarter** was secretly one of my favorite spots in Rome. While it's no Pantheon or Colosseum, it's narrow cobblestone streets and charming architecture, complete with ivy growing on the walls and flowers blooming on the balconies really made this area feel magical to me. Truthfully, I don't even quite remember where exactly I was in the Jewish Quarter so it's entirely possible that I'm over-embellishing. Maybe it was the high of being in Rome… or the wine. Either way, it felt magical to me and I hope it does for you too.

## Must-Try Food and Drinks

And it wouldn't be a trip to Rome without the food, of course. Eating Italian food was probably what I looked forward to the most on this trip. Here are some of the foods (and drinks) you MUST try while you're here:

- Pizza… duh. Classic roman-style pizza has a thick crispy crust and is absolutely delicious. I prefer a classic margherita pizza but there are many to choose from, including a Nutella pizza for dessert.
- Cacio e Pepe
- Carbonara
- Fried artichokes
- Cannoli
- Gelato
- Lots of wine
- Aperol Spritz
- Lemoncello

![Best carbonara](/images/rome-carbonara.jpg)

If that's not enough for one trip, I'm always a big fan of tours and activities as well. I recommend taking a pasta making class if you can. It was so much fun and surprisingly easier than I thought it would be. I also did a food and drinking tour. While they were fun, I think I might prefer Spanish tapas tours instead, though.

All in all, Rome was an incredible adventure and I'm glad I'm finally able to say I've been to mainland Italy. I look forward to going back in the future and exploring other parts of Italy as well.
