Our very last trip before we moved back to the US from Spain was a day trip to Gibraltar. This had been on our list for a long time, but we never seemed to have the time with all of our other travel plans. Gibraltar is located in a very unique location at one of the southern-most points in Spain, but it is not considered part of Spain. Due to its strategic location at the mouth of the Mediterranean Sea, it is a sought after territory that was ultimately taken from the Spanish by the British and is considered a British Overseas Territory. Because of this, it has a very unique blend of Spanish and British culture. I can't tell you how weird it was going to an english-speaking country after living in Europe for over a year and a half and being surrounded by foreign languages. What made it even more surreal was that it was only about an hour and a half drive from where we lived in Spain.

## Getting to Gibraltar

Getting to Gibraltar was a very interesting experience. Since it's a British Territory, you are crossing the border and have to go through border patrol (thanks Brexit), so don't forget to bring your passport! The city is pretty small and has very limited parking, so I suggest parking your car on the Spanish side in La Linea de la Concepcion and walking across the border instead. It's a very walkable city, but also very hilly so be sure to wear good walking shoes. One of the craziest parts about getting to Gibraltar was how we got from the border checkpoint to the city. They have a giant walkway for pedestrians that goes directly across an active runway for the Gibraltar airport. Of course, pedestrian access is timed around departures and arrivals so no one is out there dodging airplanes. I don't think many people can say they've walked across an active runway before, though!

## Top Things to Do in One Day

1. The Rock of Gibraltar – Don't forget to say 'Hi' to the Barbary macaques!
2. WWII Tunnels
3. Windsor Suspension bridge
4. Skywalk
5. The Pillars of Hercules
6. Explore the city center
7. Europa Point
8. Eat at a local pub

I think one of the biggest allures of coming to Gibraltar is to see the Rock. It's funny to be called a rock when it seems more like a giant mountain surrounded by water, and it feels like an island even though it's connected by land (in the form of a runway), but anything feels like a mountain when you're climbing uphill on foot! Speaking of which, unless you're a hiking enthusiast, I don't recommend climbing up the Rock on foot. It is quite the trek and will probably consume too much of your day. Ideally, you can take the cable car to the top and walk around on foot from there. Unfortunately, the day we visited was extra windy so the cable car wasn't an option, but it looked like an incredibly beautiful way to get to the top. Instead, we took a guided taxi van tour of the Rock, which we happened to enjoy more than we thought we would. We were able to get a more personal experience with a local as our guide than we would have had doing it solo.

![Barbary macaque with her baby](/living-bountifully/images/gibraltar-monkey-baby.jpg)

One of my favorite parts of the experience was seeing the wild monkeys—called Barbary macaques—that live on the Rock. We even got to see a couple babies! The best part was when the tour guide coaxed one of the monkeys onto my shoulder with a treat. That's an experience I'll never forget! Fun fact: "The Barbary macaques in Gibraltar aren't originally from Europe—they're native to North Africa. It's believed they were brought to Gibraltar by the Moors or possibly the British, and today they're the only wild monkey population living freely in Europe."

![Monkey on my head](/living-bountifully/images/gibraltar-monkey-head.jpg)

While you're on the Rock, there are some other notable sites you should try to make time for if you can. The WWII Tunnels from the war are pretty extensive throughout the rock. There are peep holes throughout the tunnels that look out into the surrounding areas, and it's really quite interesting to learn how the tunnels were utilized during the war.

![WWII Tunnels](/living-bountifully/images/gibraltar-tunnels.jpg)

There is also a suspension bridge that looks thrilling if you're not afraid of heights, but because of the weather conditions, we weren't able to walk across it. If that's not your cup of tea, then the skywalk, a lookout area on the far side of the Rock with a glass floor, might be a better alternative. The Pillars of Hercules is another stunning lookout that gives you views of Morocco in Africa (just under 8 nautical miles away!), which is as close to Africa as I've been thus far!

It's also really fun to walk around and explore the city center. There are lots of shops you can wander in and beautiful architecture along the way. There's also Europa Point on the far end of Gibraltar, but it wasn't very walkable to get there so we skipped it.

![British phone booth in Gibraltar](/living-bountifully/images/gibraltar-phone-booth.jpg)

I have yet to travel to England, so I was determined to get fish and chips while I was here. After tiring ourselves out on the Rock we went to a pub called The Lord Nelson, and it was divine! We went during a football (soccer) game and it was pretty crowded inside, so we opted to sit outside on their patio. The fish and chips were delicious and the beer was cold and crisp, it really hit the spot after a long day of walking.

![Fish and chips at The Lord Nelson](/living-bountifully/images/gibraltar-fish-chips.jpg)

I'm so glad we were able to squeeze in this day trip before we left. We had a great time exploring all that Gibraltar has to offer, which is a lot more than you would think by its size! I highly recommend making the trip if you're traveling through Southern Spain.
